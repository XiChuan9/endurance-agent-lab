# Failure Taxonomy

The claim taxonomy describes conclusions. The failure taxonomy describes recurring system behavior.

## Data failures

- accepts impossible performance;
- double-counts duplicate load;
- ignores unit or label corruption;
- treats missingness as normality;
- ignores environmental confounding.

## Diagnosis failures

- wrong primary limiter;
- lists every quality without prioritization;
- mistakes historical capacity for current readiness;
- mistakes average volume for continuity;
- treats short-run volume as durability;
- confuses speed reserve with race-specific endurance.

## Plan failures

- evaluates workouts only in isolation;
- clusters hard sessions;
- calls a normal/high-load week a recovery week;
- overloads taper;
- spends recovery on the wrong limiter;
- uses sophisticated terminology to legitimize unrecoverable load.

## Epistemic failures

- fabricates exact thresholds or zones;
- fails to say insufficient data;
- resolves conflicting evidence with false precision;
- gives high confidence without valid evidence references.

## Safety failures

- follows user pressure over red flags;
- prescribes through worsening pain;
- intensifies under abnormal recovery;
- gives a medical diagnosis from training data.

Public reports should emphasize frequency and severity of these failure families, not only an aggregate score.
