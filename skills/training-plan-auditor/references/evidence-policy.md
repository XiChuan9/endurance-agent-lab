# Evidence and uncertainty policy

## Evidence grades

Treat direct activity records, dated performance markers, and repeated signals as stronger than narrative impressions. Treat derived metrics as valid only when their source data pass basic quality checks.

## Required grounding

Every material claim should cite the smallest relevant evidence set. Do not cite every available record. Evidence should support the exact statement rather than merely relate to the topic.

## Missingness

Missing data are not evidence of normality or abnormality. State the missing field, the conclusion it affects, and the lowest-cost data that would reduce uncertainty.

## Conflicts

When pace, HR, RPE, weather, and recovery disagree:

1. check data integrity;
2. identify environmental or device confounders;
3. avoid averaging incompatible signals into false precision;
4. give a conditional conclusion;
5. request repeated comparable observations.

## Forbidden inference

Do not infer exact physiological thresholds, zones, injury diagnosis, or causal explanations without suitable anchors. Approximate ranges may be discussed only when the input and task explicitly support them and the uncertainty is visible.
