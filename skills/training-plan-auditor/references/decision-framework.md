# Endurance plan decision framework

## Adaptation allocation

A training plan is an allocation of limited adaptation and recovery capacity. Diagnose the highest-value constraint before selecting stimuli.

A practical hierarchy is:

1. safety and ability to train consistently;
2. continuity and recoverable volume;
3. event-relevant durability;
4. threshold and race-specific endurance;
5. speed/VO2max support where it is genuinely limiting;
6. taper and race execution.

This is not a universal periodization law. It is a decision order for avoiding high-cost work that does not solve the present bottleneck.

## Current fitness versus historical capacity

Historical PBs establish demonstrated capacity, not current readiness. Use them to identify speed reserve and goal relation, while using recent continuity and race-relevant exposure to judge current preparation.

## Volume versus continuity

Average weekly volume can hide long gaps and spikes. Inspect the sequence, coefficient of variation, zero weeks, and week-over-week progression. Consistent moderate exposure may be more useful than the same average produced by alternating overload and inactivity.

## Durability

Durability means preserving output and physiological control as duration accumulates. Relevant evidence includes long-run exposure, late-race fade, pace/HR drift, and the conversion of short-distance performance to longer events. Avoid equating a single long run with durable readiness.

## Global weekly architecture

Count the recovery cost of all sessions together. Threshold, VO2max, race pace, long fast work, hills, and heavy strength may each be reasonable in isolation. Their interaction determines whether the week is coherent.

## Short horizons

When race day is close, the system cannot rebuild every fitness quality. Reduce fatigue and preserve small, familiar doses of event-relevant intensity. Do not create fitness debt in a taper.
